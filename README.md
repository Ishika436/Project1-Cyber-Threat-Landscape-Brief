# Project 1: Cyber Threat Landscape Brief

## Overview
The **Cyber Threat Landscape Brief** is a cybersecurity research and awareness project that examines common cyber threats faced by college students in their everyday digital activities.

Students regularly use emails, learning platforms, social media, cloud storage, mobile applications, and public Wi-Fi. While these technologies make academic activities easier, they also expose users to various cybersecurity risks.

This brief explores five common cyber threats, explaining how they work, how to identify them, and the steps users can take to protect themselves.

## Objectives
- Understand common cybersecurity threats affecting students.
- Study the attack methods used by cybercriminals.
- Identify warning signs and indicators of potential attacks.
- Examine cybersecurity reports and advisories from trusted sources.
- Recommend practical preventive measures to improve digital safety.

## Threats Covered

| No. | Cyber Threat | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Phishing and Scams | Fake messages, malicious links and credential theft |
| 2 | Malware and Fake Apps | Malicious downloads, spyware and unsafe applications |
| 3 | Weak/Repeated Passwords | Credential abuse and account compromise |
| 4 | Ransomware | File encryption, data loss and ransom demands |
| 5 | Unsecure Public Wi-Fi | Fake hotspots and information interception |

## Key Areas of Research

Each threat is examined through the following aspects:

- **Description:** Explanation of the threat.
- **Attack Method:** How the attack can occur.
- **Indicators:** Warning signs that may indicate an attack.
- **Evidence:** Supporting statistics, cybersecurity reports and advisories.
- **Prevention:** Practical steps to reduce the risk.

## Research Sources

The brief refers to cybersecurity publications and advisories from:

- Verizon – 2025 Data Breach Investigations Report (DBIR).
- CERT-In – Preventing Online Scams.
- CERT-In – Securing Mobile Devices and Applications.
- Verizon – 2025 DBIR Asia-Pacific Findings.
- CERT-In – Cybersecurity Measures.

## Key Takeaways

The research highlights the importance of:
- Recognising phishing attempts and suspicious links.
- Downloading applications only from trusted sources.
- Using strong, unique passwords and Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
- Maintaining regular backups of important files.
- Taking precautions when connecting to public Wi-Fi.

## Conclusion

Cybersecurity awareness is an important part of responsible digital usage. Understanding common attack methods and adopting basic security practices can help students protect their accounts, personal information and academic work.
